The key distinction:

- `V(s)` estimates how good a position is under a policy.
- `Q(s, hit)` and `Q(s, stand)` estimate how good the two actions are, with later behavior also following a policy.
- The learner now uses those action estimates to change its decisions between hands.

The Greek epsilon controls exploration. At epsilon 0.1 with one best action, the best action gets 95% probability and the alternative gets 5%, because random exploration may choose either action. During evaluation the greedy policy has no exploration and the table is frozen.

## One hand-worked update

Suppose hitting in a position has two samples, +1 and -1. Its value is zero. Another hit sample returns -1:

```text
new count = 3
Q(hit) = 0 + (-1 - 0)/3 = -1/3
```

The stand estimate is unchanged. We update once for each state-action pair's first occurrence in a completed hand. This is Monte Carlo control, not the separate Q-learning algorithm.

## First measured result

Training: 50,000 hands, seed 7, epsilon 0.1. Evaluation: 20,000 hands per policy, environment seed 1,000,007.

| Policy | Mean return per hand | Approximate 95% sampling interval |
|---|---:|---|
| greedy | -0.06935 | [-0.08260, -0.05610] |
| threshold | -0.08195 | [-0.09503, -0.06887] |
| random | -0.38865 | [-0.40112, -0.37618] |

The learned policy's sample mean was better than the threshold baseline in this run, but that is preliminary. One training seed and individual sampling intervals do not establish general superiority. These intervals account for evaluation outcomes only, not uncertainty from training different agents. A persistent finite shoe would require a different independence analysis.

All policies use the same evaluation seed, but different action sequences draw different numbers of cards. These are not paired identical hands. The policies were evaluated without training on these outcomes, but we must avoid repeatedly tuning against this same evaluation set and later calling it a fresh final test.

A useful caution appears in the learned table: hard 16 against 10 selects stand because its estimate is -0.56989 versus -0.58314 for hit. That narrow, noisy difference is not proof that stand is optimal. At hard 20 against 10, the estimates are +0.43670 for stand (2,038 visits) and -0.80000 for hit (130 visits). Read counts alongside values, and do not mistake the table for a certified strategy chart.

## 04:00 follow-up: five seeds, unchanged settings

The replication is now complete: training seeds **7, 19, 42, 101, 2026**, 50,000 training hands each, epsilon 0.1. Each final policy was frozen and evaluated on 20,000 hands at environment seed `1,000,000 + training seed`. The threshold and random baselines were evaluated at that same evaluation seed. The seed-7 run repeats the earlier sample; it is counted once, not as an extra independent result.

| Training seed | Learned greedy mean | Threshold mean | Random mean |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 7 | -0.06935 | -0.08195 | -0.38865 |
| 19 | -0.08550 | -0.07725 | -0.40630 |
| 42 | -0.07310 | -0.08205 | -0.40410 |

| 2026 | -0.06375 | -0.07215 | -0.39885 |
| Mean across five runs | -0.07400 | -0.07970 | -0.39771 |

The learned agent was worse than threshold in **seed 19**. That run is retained, not hidden or retuned away. Across runs its observed mean ranged from -0.08550 to -0.06375. The sample standard deviation of its five run means was **0.00834** units/hand.

This standard deviation combines variation between trained policies **and** finite evaluation sampling. It does not isolate training uncertainty. Each run's individual sampling interval remains in the [saved replication record](../../experiments/2026-09-25-control-multiseed.json). The fixed baselines vary too, because different evaluation seeds sample different games.

The observed average advantage over threshold is `-0.07400 - (-0.07970) = +0.00570` units per hand. We have not established that this small advantage is statistically reliable. Five runs, with the existing evaluation budgets and no rule-matched optimal reference, do not establish optimality or consistent superiority. The learned player also remains negative-return in these samples. The comparison with random is much larger descriptively, but this lesson is about honest measurement rather than selecting a favorable claim.
