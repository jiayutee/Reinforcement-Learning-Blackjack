# 16 October: collect an episode before learning from it

## Prerequisites

A policy maps an observation to an action. A transition records observation, action, reward, next observation and whether the round ended. An episode here is one whole blackjack round, including every split hand. Collecting experience is separate from changing a value table or training a network.

The new [collector](../../public_game/episodes.py) connects a policy callback to the public-rule session. It never passes the private shoe or dealer hole card to that callback. Existing Foundations agents are still not compatible public-rule policies.

## Intuition and equations

We chose zero intermediate rewards and final normalized round profit R=P/b. With gamma1, return G_t=sum of rewards from time t to termination. For split, double, stand yielding combined profit20 on initial stake20, rewards are[0,0,1], so each transition's return is1. That shared outcome can later train Monte Carlo estimates; the collector itself performs no updates.

Do not reward the first finished split hand separately and then reward aggregate profit again. That would count some results twice. A snapshot refresh also generates no transition.

## Coding steps

1. Deal through the authoritative session, keeping its shoe between rounds.
2. If a natural immediately settles, record the outcome with an empty transition list and `opening_terminal=True`. No player decision occurred; do not invent hit or stand.
3. Give the policy a deep copy of the visible snapshot. Its accidental edits cannot alter the stored observation or game state.
4. Require its selected action to belong to the advertised legal actions.
5. Apply it with the current revision. Record zero reward while active and normalized profit once on terminal completion.
6. Return transition records plus round outcome. The dictionaries remain caller-editable data, not immutable archives; they are detached from the session.

Opening naturals still count in round-level evaluation even though their action-transition list is empty. Averaging only rounds with decisions would change the population being measured. A future learner must explicitly handle this distinction.

## Runnable example

From the repository root:

```sh
python3 - <<'PYCODE'
from public_game.session import PublicSession
from public_game.episodes import collect_round
result = collect_round(PublicSession(seed=7), lambda observation: 'stand')
print([(t.action, t.reward, t.done) for t in result.transitions])
print(result.normalized_profit)
PYCODE
```

Expected from the already-verified seed7 round: one stand transition with reward-1 and doneTrue, round outcome-1. This fixed policy does not learn and is not recommended strategy.
