# 7 October: settlement is not the same as reward

## Prerequisites

A stake is committed before play. Credit is the amount returned afterward, including returned stake. Profit is the change relative to that stake. An RL reward should reflect the declared objective, not an accidentally mixed accounting quantity. Review [the finite-shoe lesson](2026-10-06.md) and [public-round contract](../decisions/public-round-v1.md).

## Exact arithmetic

All money here means virtual chips. Store integer half-chip units: 10 chips = 20 units. For stake w already debited:

- Loss: credit=0; profit=0-w=-w.
- Push: credit=w; profit=w-w=0.
- Ordinary win: credit=2w; profit=2w-w=w.
- Eligible natural: credit=5w/2; profit=3w/2.

Require even positive units for a whole-chip stake. Then 3:2 natural payouts are integers without floating-point rounding. Example: stake 20 units, natural credit 50, profit 30. In chips: stake 10, credit 25, profit 15.

A split-hand 21 is ordinary: at the same stake it credits 40 units and profits 20. A dealer natural beats ordinary 21; equal eligible naturals push. Player bust loses even if the dealer has not drawn to 17, because no comparison is needed.

## Code walkthrough

[public_game/settlement.py](../../public_game/settlement.py) adds a pure `settle_hand` function:

1. Validate positive even integer stake and at least two cards per hand.
2. Reuse `hand_facts` to identify totals, busts and natural eligibility.
3. Resolve player bust, then dealer natural, then eligible player natural.
4. For an ordinary comparison, require the dealer to have reached 17 or busted.
5. Compute immutable outcome/stake/credit/profit facts, with profit=credit-stake.

It does not mutate balances, draw cards, choose actions or credit a result. The future round engine must ensure the player finished acting and that the result is credited once. Calling the pure function twice returns the same facts; adding its credit to a balance twice would still be an engine bug. An active player's total does not reveal whether they chose stand, so this function cannot enforce that lifecycle by itself.

## Worked split/double example

Initial wager 10 chips (20 units). Split costs another 20 units; double the first hand adds 20 more. Final stakes are 40 and 20 units. First hand wins, second loses:

1. Total stake=60 units.
2. Credits=80+0=80 units.
3. Net profit=(80-40)+(0-20)=20 units.
4. With starting balance 200 units, ending balance=200-60+80=220.
5. Normalized round reward=profit/initial wager=20/20=+1.

Do not use credit 80 as reward: that counts returned stake as gain. Other split/double outcomes can yield normalized rewards outside [-1,1], so the Foundations evaluator must not be reused without adapting its assumptions.

## 04:00 checkpoint: prevent exhaustion before a round starts

Added `PUBLIC_ROUND_RESERVE=101` and `Shoe.prepare_round(minimum_cards=...)`. The [round contract](../decisions/public-round-v1.md#reserve-derivation-7-october) now includes the proof:

- A player can hit only below 21. Before its last hit, the sum with aces at 1 is at most 20, so there can be at most 20 cards. One more gives a conservative maximum of 21 cards per final hand.
- Four final hands consume at most 4×21=84 cards. Splitting redistributes cards rather than discarding them.
- The dealer draws only below 17: at most 16 cards before the last draw, hence 17 afterward.
- Total upper bound = 84+17 = **101**, including initial cards. This is deliberately loose, not a reachable maximum claim.

The future engine must call `shoe.prepare_round(PUBLIC_ROUND_RESERVE)` between rounds and enforce those stopping rules. Six decks at 75% penetration nominally leave only 78 cards, so the reserve can trigger an earlier reshuffle. Generic shoe experiments preserve the default zero-reserve behavior. No draw-time refill was introduced.

All **93 Python tests passed**. Added checks for exactly 101 cards (no reserve shuffle), 100 cards (shuffle even before the cut), invalid reserve nonmutation and rejecting a reserve larger than full-shoe capacity. A single deck cannot satisfy this conservative 101-card requirement; that is not a claim that single-deck blackjack is impossible.

## 05:00 checkpoint: the opening deal is a state transition

[Opening code](../../public_game/opening.py) now combines wager validation, deal order and natural settlement in a pure function. **Pure** means it returns a result without changing its inputs or an external balance. A frozen Python dataclass groups named results and prevents normal reassignment; tuples retain the dealt cards even if the caller later clears its input list.

Think of a transition as `old state + event -> new state`. Here the event is four dealt cards, in player/dealer/player/dealer order. The starting balance B and wager w are measured in half-chip units, as in the earlier examples.

1. Check B is a nonnegative integer and w is a positive even integer no larger than B. B itself may be odd after a natural payout. The future session must call `validate_wager` **before** touching the shoe.
2. Group cards 0 and 2 as the player hand; cards 1 and 3 as the dealer hand. Python indexes start at zero.
3. Reserve the wager: provisional balance = B-w.
4. If either hand has a natural, reuse `settle_hand` and immediately enter `settled`. Final balance = B-w+credit. This check happens before any opportunity to hit, split or double.
5. Otherwise enter `player_turn`, with balance B-w and settlement `None`. `None` means no completed result yet, not a zero-profit push.

For B=200, w=20 and player A+K versus dealer 10+6, credit=50, final balance=200-20+50=230, and net profit=30. No dealer draw is needed for this natural. With player 10+7 versus dealer A+6, balance=180 and play is still pending. The temporary balance decrease is committed stake; it is not a completed loss.
