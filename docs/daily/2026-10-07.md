# 7 October: settlement is not the same as reward

## Prerequisites

A stake is committed before play. Credit is the amount returned afterward, including returned stake. Profit is the change relative to that stake. An RL reward should reflect the declared objective, not an accidentally mixed accounting quantity. Review [the finite-shoe lesson](2026-10-06.md) and [public-round contract](../decisions/public-round-v1.md).

## Exact arithmetic

All money here means virtual chips. Store integer half-chip units: 10 chips = 20 units. For stake w already debited:

- Loss: credit=0; profit=0-w=-w.
- Push: credit=w; profit=w-w=0.
- Ordinary win: credit=2w; profit=2w-w=w.
- Eligible natural: credit=5w/2; profit=3w/2.

Require even positive units for a whole-chip stake. Then 3:2 natural payouts are integers without floating-point rounding. Example: stake 20 units, natural credit 50, profit 30. In chips: stake 10, credit 25, profit 15.

A split-hand 21 is ordinary: at the same stake it credits 40 units and profits 20. A dealer natural beats ordinary 21; equal eligible naturals push. Player bust loses even if the dealer has not drawn to 17, because no comparison is needed.

## Code walkthrough

[public_game/settlement.py](../../public_game/settlement.py) adds a pure `settle_hand` function:

1. Validate positive even integer stake and at least two cards per hand.
2. Reuse `hand_facts` to identify totals, busts and natural eligibility.
3. Resolve player bust, then dealer natural, then eligible player natural.
4. For an ordinary comparison, require the dealer to have reached 17 or busted.
5. Compute immutable outcome/stake/credit/profit facts, with profit=credit-stake.

It does not mutate balances, draw cards, choose actions or credit a result. The future round engine must ensure the player finished acting and that the result is credited once. Calling the pure function twice returns the same facts; adding its credit to a balance twice would still be an engine bug. An active player's total does not reveal whether they chose stand, so this function cannot enforce that lifecycle by itself.

## Worked split/double example

Initial wager 10 chips (20 units). Split costs another 20 units; double the first hand adds 20 more. Final stakes are 40 and 20 units. First hand wins, second loses:

1. Total stake=60 units.
2. Credits=80+0=80 units.
3. Net profit=(80-40)+(0-20)=20 units.
4. With starting balance 200 units, ending balance=200-60+80=220.
5. Normalized round reward=profit/initial wager=20/20=+1.

Do not use credit 80 as reward: that counts returned stake as gain. Other split/double outcomes can yield normalized rewards outside [-1,1], so the Foundations evaluator must not be reused without adapting its assumptions.
