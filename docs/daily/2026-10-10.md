# 10 October: keep the reward denominator fixed

## Prerequisites and intuition

A stake is the amount committed to a hand. Profit is the final balance change, excluding returned stake. A normalized profit expresses that change in units of the original wager, allowing differently sized wagers to be compared.

Doubling changes the final stake. It must not change the denominator used to measure the round. Otherwise a doubled loss looks just like an ordinary loss, hiding its extra cost from learning and evaluation.

## Equations and worked example

Let b be the initial wager, w the final stake and P the net profit. The completed round metric is R=P/b.

1. Start with 200 half-chip units and b=20.
2. Deal debits 20, leaving 180.
3. Double debits another 20: w=40 and balance=160.
4. A win credits 80, leaving 240. P=240-200=40.
5. R=40/20=+2. Dividing by final stake would incorrectly give 40/40=+1 for this chosen round objective.

A double loss has P=-40 and R=-2. A push has R=0. An eligible natural has P=1.5b and R=1.5. These are game outcomes, not predictions or promises about average performance.

With gamma=1 and reward assigned only at round completion, episodic return is the sum of intermediate zeros and this terminal R. For future split rounds, sum all hand profits first, then divide by the same initial wager. The existing Foundations reward bounds and evaluation formula cannot be reused unchanged.

## Coding step by step

[OpeningRound](../../public_game/opening.py) now saves `initial_stake` when the deal starts. The existing `stake` field still tracks the current hand's stake. The session's immutable replacement on double changes `stake` and preserves `initial_stake`.

[PublicSession.view](../../public_game/session.py) exposes both fields and `normalized_profit`. Before settlement this metric is `None`, meaning unfinished, rather than a completed push. Once settled it is `profit / initial_stake`. A new deal stores its own denominator and clears the previous result.

This is a snapshot metric, **not a reward event on every read**. A future RL adapter must emit the terminal reward once for the accepted transition. Refreshing a snapshot must not generate another training transition. No adapter or agent has been connected yet.
