## 04:00 checkpoint: same games, different estimates

Ran MC and TD with alpha 0.1 and 0.05 for each of five seeds (7, 19, 42, 101, 2026), 10,000 hands per run. These settings were chosen before inspecting results. No settings were tuned afterward. [Saved results](../../experiments/2026-09-26-prediction-comparison.json) include all three lesson states and source provenance.

For **hard 20 against dealer 10**, the final estimates were:

| Seed | Visits | MC | TD alpha 0.1 | TD alpha 0.05 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 7 | 507 | +0.422091 | +0.413449 | +0.456187 |
| 19 | 518 | +0.465251 | +0.627659 | +0.572105 |
| 42 | 533 | +0.437148 | +0.187019 | +0.298712 |
| 101 | 529 | +0.436673 | +0.086610 | +0.281109 |
| 2026 | 508 | +0.405512 | +0.249314 | +0.237941 |

At total 20 the policy stands, so every update is terminal. Here TD's difference from MC is entirely its constant learning rate versus MC's sample mean: there is **no bootstrap term at this state**. With alpha 0.1, repeated updates give old information a multiplier of 0.9 each time; after ten updates its weight is 0.9^10, about 0.349. Alpha 0.05 retains about 0.599 after ten updates. This explains sensitivity to recent outcomes without claiming one setting is more accurate.

The soft-13-against-2 state had only 6–14 visits across these runs. Small TD estimates near zero can reflect initialization and slow propagation, not certainty that the true value is zero. MC's sampled returns are also noisy. Without a trusted value reference, this experiment cannot rank accuracy, measure bias, or establish that either method is better. Five runs show variability, not a full uncertainty analysis.

All outcome summaries were asserted identical within each seed. Mean returns by seed were -0.0913, -0.0803, -0.0627, -0.0894, and -0.0839. The same fixed player generated 50,000 distinct seeded hands, replayed across three estimators (150,000 simulated hands total); treating these as 15 independent samples would exaggerate evidence.
