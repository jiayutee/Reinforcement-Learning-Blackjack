# 11 October: aggregate hand results into one round reward

## Prerequisites and intuition

A split creates several hands inside one round. Each hand has a stake, a credit returned at settlement and a net profit. The initial wager belongs to the entire round. Yesterday's lesson established why that denominator stays fixed even after extra stakes.

A round can contain both winners and losers. Calling the whole round a win because one hand won loses information. Instead, sum the money outcomes. The new [round settlement helper](../../public_game/round_settlement.py) does this without changing any balance or drawing cards.

## Equations and worked example

For each completed hand i, let w_i be its final stake and c_i its credit. Its profit is p_i=c_i-w_i. Then:

- Total stake W=sum(w_i).
- Total credit C=sum(c_i).
- Round profit P=sum(p_i)=C-W.
- Normalized round result R=P/b, where b is the original wager.

Start at 200 units with original wager20. Split and double the first hand: stakes40 and20, total60. Against dealer18, first hand19 wins and second17 loses. Credits are80 and0; profits are+40 and-20. Thus C=80, P=20 and R=20/20=+1. Final balance is200-60+80=220. Apply total credit once; do not debit stakes again.

Four doubled winning hands would yield R=+8; four doubled losses yield -8. These are accounting bounds illustrated with supplied hands, not probabilities or claims that a strategy earns those returns. The Foundations [-1,1] evaluator remains incompatible.

## Code walkthrough

1. `settle_round` checks a positive even initial wager and one through four completed hand records.
2. Each final stake must be the original wager or twice it under our current double rules.
3. Reuse `settle_hand` with each hand's split context, preserving ordinary payout for split21.
4. Collect immutable per-hand settlements, then sum their stake, credit and profit fields.
5. Expose normalized profit using the original wager.

All hands must be marked complete. Those flags do not prove legal play by themselves: the future session still owns legal actions, turn order and dealer progression. An all-bust round can settle against the existing dealer cards without additional draws; ordinary comparisons require a finished dealer.
